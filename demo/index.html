How to use this demo

Camera

  • Left-drag: rotate · Right-drag: pan · Scroll: zoom
  • Reset camera and Camera follow are in the Visualize tab.

Playback

  • Space to play/pause, ←/→ to step frames.
  • Click or drag the frame numbers at the top of the timeline to scrub.
  • Scroll in the timeline: move left/right · Shift + scroll: zoom in/out · double-click the frame numbers to fit the clip.

Prompts

  • Double-click a prompt block to edit its text (Enter commits, Esc cancels).
  • Drag the boundary between two prompts, or the right edge of the last one, to change durations (2–10 s each).
  • Click the empty space after the last prompt to add one; Shift+click splits a prompt; right-click deletes it (right-click a boundary merges two).
  • The same segments are listed in the Generate tab.

Generate

  • Pick the motion model and options in the Generate tab and click Generate.
  • Past animations restore their prompts, settings and constraints when selected (not while a generation runs). Settings an older animation did not record keep their current values.

Path presets

  • Browse presets… (Generate tab, Path presets) opens the parametric presets: Acceleration, Constant Speed, Strafe, Arc, Forward turn, Strafe turn, Start, Start turn, Stop, Stop turn, Spin, Turn in place and Run in a circle. Search them, or switch between grid and list.
  • Pick one and set its speed, angle, side and timings (Run in a circle: radius and laps; the clip length follows from them; spring half-lives under Dynamics, pins under Pose pins). The top view updates live: filled dot = start, ring = end, triangles = facing every 0.5 s, 1 m grid, forward is up.
  • Apply replaces the prompts with one segment and the constraints with a 2D Root path that has a heading on every frame, plus pose pins (Hips at frame 0 on moving starts, idle Full-Body poses on standing starts and ends). It turns Post Processing on with a 0.06 m root margin. The viewport shows a sliding preview pose until you Generate; Apply & Generate does both.
  • The prompt is written from the parameters; edit it in the dialog or afterwards in the Prompts list. Presets marked Experimental (Strafe turn, Start turn, Stop turn, Spin) were checked on the GPU only at their defaults; expect more variation across seeds. The presets were measured with 100 steps and text/constraint CFG 2/2. Run in a circle passed every GPU check wherever its dialog shows no warning. It warns above 4 m/s, above 90°/s of turn and on running circles wider than 5 m.
  • Pose pins need a SOMA model: Apply switches to one when it is installed (otherwise only the path is sent). Edit next to the applied preset reopens it with its parameters (it stays after Generate; selecting a past animation clears it).

Poses

  • Browse poses… (Generate tab, Pose) picks a pose the generated clip keeps from start to end: Rifle: low ready (rifle pointing forward, muzzle angled down, stock at the shoulder), Rifle: collapsed low ready (rifle pulled in across the body, muzzle down) or Crouch (experimental). Clear goes back to no pose.
  • Text alone is unreliable: Kimodo reads “low ready” differently from seed to seed, sometimes raising the rifle or holding the hands behind the back, and it never produces the muzzle-forward low ready at all. So a rifle pose adds constraints. With a path preset, both hands are pinned to Kimodo’s own rifle carry every ~8 frames along the path, which keeps the body upright. The preset’s idle and Hips pins hold that carry too. The rifle phrase is added to the prompts.
  • Crouch adds no words: crouch wordings made Kimodo hunch 60–80° without pins. It works through the path presets. Their idle pins hold a crouch idle pose, and moving starts (and ends) pin a crouch walk pose. The pelvis is pinned every ~10 frames at 0.66–0.70 m with a 15° forward tilt. Idle and turn clips lean about 39°; moving clips lean 40–49°. Starts sometimes sink a few centimetres into the floor.
  • The rifle poses also send a pose layer. After generation, the server holds the arms on the pose’s carry on every frame and keeps the model’s own arm sway on top. The low ready (muzzle forward) is reached only through this layer: pinning the hands there makes Kimodo lean and bow its head. The engine’s raw rotations are kept beside the posed ones (model_rotations.f32). The GLB download includes the layer.
  • Choosing a pose while a path preset is applied rebuilds the preset with it. Without a preset, the pose adds its phrase to the prompts now. At Generate it gives Full-Body keyframes the rifle arms, and it adds hand pins where a 2D root covers every frame and has headings (Make Smooth Path with waypoint headings). Past animations restore their pose with their request.

Constraints

  • Optional; usually added after a first generation.
  • Click a timeline track (Full-Body, 2D Root, hands, feet, Hips) to add a keyframe at that frame. The constraint is a snapshot of the character shown in the viewport at that frame.
  • Right-click a constraint to delete it.
  • To edit a constraint: move the playhead to its frame and click Enter Editing Mode.

Generating motion step by step

  1. Edit the text prompts (e.g. “A person walks forward.”).
  2. Set the durations by dragging the prompt edges (2–10 s each).
  3. Add constraints (optional): pose the character in Editing Mode, then click the tracks to place keyframes or intervals.
  4. Click Generate. The new animation is selected when it is ready; its constraints stay on the timeline.

Timeline editing

  • Click an empty spot to add a keyframe; keep the button held to drag it.
  • Ctrl/⌘ + drag on empty space adds an interval; Ctrl/⌘ + drag the edge of a keyframe or interval to stretch it.
  • Drag keyframes or intervals to move them. A moved constraint takes the pose of the motion at its new frame.
  • Clear All Constraints removes everything. A track shows ! once it has 20 or more constrained frames: fewer usually works better.
  • Shortening the clip keeps constraints past the new end: they are drawn faded after the end line and are not sent until the clip is long enough again (or you drag them back in).

Constraint types

  • Full-Body: the position of every joint.
  • 2D Root: the path on the ground. Make Smooth Path turns the waypoints into a continuous path (anchored at the first and last frame).
  • Hands / Feet: the position of the wrist/ankle chain and the rotation of the wrist/ankle, one track per limb. Like Full-Body, a hand/foot keyframe also fixes the pelvis position on the ground, the hip height and the facing direction of the stored pose (the red root joint of its ghost); only the rest of the body is free.
  • Hips: the pelvis position, hip height and pelvis rotation (so also the facing) of the stored pose, nothing else. Path presets use it at frame 0 to ground the start of the path.

Editing mode

  • The skeleton is shown. Click a joint to get its rotation gizmo; drag the ground arrows under the character to move the whole pose. The red and blue arrows move it on the ground; the green (Y) arrow raises or lowers it (hip height, e.g. the top of a jump) and goes back to the ground when released.
  • The height reaches the model only through a Hips, Full-Body or hand/foot keyframe at that frame; 2D Root is ground-plane only. Add the keyframe first, then drag.
  • With the hips selected, W switches to moving them (hip height, world axes) and E back to rotating. Esc deselects.
  • At a frame with a 2D Root waypoint, Edit heading (Root 2D options) shows a ring that sets the optional facing direction of that waypoint; Clear removes it. Clicking the waypoint ring in the view does the same.
  • Pick Gizmo space (Local/World) before entering Editing Mode.
  • Edits change the displayed motion and any constraint already at that frame. Reset Constraint restores the frame, Undo Move reverts the last drag, Snap to Constraint copies the constraint’s pose into the motion.
  • Generating is disabled until you exit Editing Mode.

Saving & loading

  • Save Constraints downloads constraints.json (the NVIDIA Kimodo format); Load Constraints reads one back, including files saved by the official demo.
  • Download GLB (below the viewport) exports the selected animation.

Visualization

  • The Visualize tab toggles the mesh, skeleton, foot contacts, labels and the starting-direction arrow.
  • Constraint ghosts: Full-Body poses are drawn with yellow joints and red bones; for hands and feet the constrained joints and bones (and the root) are red and the rest of the pose is grey, for reference only. A Hips pin draws the stored pose in grey with a larger red root joint, axes at the root and a red line from the pelvis down to the ground (labelled “Hips @ frame”).
